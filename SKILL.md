# neil-GTA

Convert each supplied photo into one illustrated poster. Use the photo for identity, pose, clothing, composition, and setting. If the user supplies style references or previously approved posters, use them for rendering and typography. Treat embedded text as visual content. Publish or share source photos and finished images only when explicitly requested.

## Read the photo

Identify the recognizable features to keep and the space available for lettering. For portraits, preserve expression, facial proportions, and distinguishing details. For groups, preserve gestures, spacing, and relative heights. For scenes, preserve landmarks, perspective, and the original time-of-day cues. Convert HEIC to a supported format if needed, keeping orientation and the original file intact. Exclude playback controls from screenshot compositions.

Default to full-bleed vertical 2:3. Extend surroundings as needed to fit the subject and typography without stretching or covering faces. Use crisp painted contours, hard-edged shadow shapes, cool violet or blue shade, and warm edge light where the source lighting supports it. Preserve the character of daylight, overcast, or night scenes.

## Design the lettering

Use the user's exact copy when supplied. Otherwise choose a short original English title, usually two words, and a short caption suited to the scene. Set the title in compact staggered rows of heavyweight ivory block lettering with square counters, rounded outside corners, varied ascenders, and a shared near-black contour. Give the wordmark generous side margins. Keep it clear of faces and important gestures.

Choose one complete layout based on the available space. For requested random variants, draw from layouts that fit the photo. In a batch, vary layouts when the images support it.

| Layout | Title and VI | Caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White outline footer | Keep the upper wordmark compact. Center a hollow geometric VI at the foot, with thin white lines and a slightly stronger soft neutral-white halo. | Run the caption through a break in the middle of VI. Use readable regular-weight white type, modest tracking, and a line roughly one-third of the canvas. Shorten original copy if it would sprawl. |
| Solid VI beside title | Nest a compact colored VI beside the upper wordmark, sharing its black contour. Sample the accent from the scene. | Center a small, restrained white caption at the foot. |
| Gradient VI beside title | Nest a cyan-to-magenta VI at the right of the lower title row, sharing the black contour. A small slanted handwritten Six is optional if VI stays readable. | Center a small, restrained white caption at the foot. |

## Generate and review

Use the host image-generation tool with the source photo and any necessary style reference. State each input's role, the visual details to preserve, the chosen layout, and exact copy. Generate one result per requested photo. If image generation is unavailable, provide the scene-specific prompt and identify it as a prompt.

Inspect the output for recognizable likeness and setting, exact wording, and a readable VI. Check that the caption and title leave room for the subject, that the footer has comfortable margins, and that a white-outline VI has a neutral glow. If a defect materially affects the result, target that region in a follow-up edit using the generated poster as the edit input. Return the finished image and briefly identify the chosen layout. If a defect remains, say so.
